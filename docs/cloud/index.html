Volume II of Introduction to LLM Engineering · a free, interactive textbook

Cloud AI Infrastructure

Serving large models at datacenter scale: the power, silicon, networks, storage, orchestration, capacity, money, reliability and security behind every token—written for mathematicians and data scientists preparing for FAANG-level infrastructure and ML-systems interviews.

Volume I explained what happens inside one inference engine: the KV cache, attention kernels, batching, quantisation and parallelism. This volume explains everything around it—how a request crosses the internet, which machine in which region answers it, how that machine was bought, powered, cooled, provisioned and kept alive, what the token cost, and how the service survives failures and attackers. It also covers the workloads Volume I left open: multimodal models, embeddings and vector search, agents with tool calls, guardrails, and batch and reinforcement-learning rollouts.

Contents

Who this volume is for

The reader is the reader of Volume I: fluent in linear algebra, probability and optimisation, experienced with data and models, new to operating systems, networks and distributed systems. Infrastructure interviews at FAANG companies and AI labs ask questions such as "design a global LLM API", "how many megawatts does this fleet need", "why did your cold start take nine minutes" and "how would you isolate tenants who share a GPU". Each has a quantitative answer, and this volume derives them. "For the mathematician" boxes translate systems concepts into the language of sets, functions, queues and optimisation.

How it relates to Volume I

The running examples are the same—Llama 3 8B, Llama 3 70B and DeepSeek-V3 on H100, H200, B200 and GB200 systems—and so is the notation, so numbers carry over: Llama 3 8B still has 16.06 GB of BF16 weights and 128 KiB of KV cache per token. Where this volume relies on an engine-level result (the decode latency model, continuous batching, disaggregation, the cost per million tokens) it cites the section of Volume I that derives it, written "Vol. I §11.8".
